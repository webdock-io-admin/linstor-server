## Problem
A rendered REST answer stays in memory until the client has read it.

Dev rack, 2026-11-10, stock 1.35.3 controller (-Xmx2G, 3.7 GiB guest, YugabyteDB co-located):
- One client opened 20 connections every 10 s. Each sent `GET /v1/view/resources` (about 14 MB, 1,970 rds) with SO_RCVBUF 4096 and never read.
- About 240 such requests were in flight from 13:04 CET.
- At 13:07:58 the guest ran out of memory. The kernel killed a YSQL backend, the HA lease was lost at 13:07:20, and the controller was fenced.
- At the kill, the controller JVM's RSS was 2.37 GiB (heap 2 GiB plus off-heap).

Fix 24 moves the render and the write off the in-order worker. It bounds the number of concurrent answers (RestResponse scheduler: 16 threads, 32 queued), not their bytes.

## Cause
- `objectMapper.writeValueAsString` renders the whole body into a String (View.java, Volumes.java, StoragePools.java).
- Grizzly's `OutputBuffer` hands the whole write to the connection's async write queue and checks `canWrite` only afterwards.
- With the listener default `maxPendingBytes = -1`, each connection's queue limit is AUTO, 4 × the socket's send buffer (`TCPNIOConnection`, Grizzly 4.0.2).
- In our test, a 4-MiB answer to a client that never reads was queued in full and the write returned at once. The body then sat in the queue until the connection closed. The transport write timeout (fix 24 D2) never fired, because no write was blocked.
- A large heap write also makes the JDK allocate a temporary direct buffer of that size per writing thread (`sun.nio.ch.Util`). That is consistent with the off-heap growth seen, but was not measured separately.
- Found later (N240, byte budget only): a budget checked after the render still renders every refused request. And Grizzly's `close()` after a write timeout is graceful, so the queued bytes and the socket stay until the client closes.

## Fix
### E2: byte budget (`ResponseBudgetFilter` + `ResponseBudget`)
- Every 2xx, non-HEAD String or byte[] entity over 1 MiB takes a lease for its UTF-8 size.
- Global cap: 1/4 of the max heap by default (1 GiB with -Xmx4G), or `LINSTOR_CONTROLLER_REST_RESPONSE_BUDGET_MIB`.
- Per client address: 64 MiB (`..._PER_CLIENT_MIB`) and 4 large answers (`..._PER_CLIENT_COUNT`).
- An answer that does not fit becomes 503 with `Retry-After: 5`, and its body is dropped. The 503 body is the usual ApiCallRc JSON with FAIL_UNKNOWN_ERROR.
- The lease is returned when the write ends, by success, IOException or write timeout.
- Safety net: a lease older than 10 minutes is returned on the next acquire and counted (`getExpiredCount`).
- The caps are logged at startup.
- Order: `@Priority(1)`, so this filter runs after the other response filters.
- No explicit Content-Length: Grizzly gzips JSON (compression ON) and frames it itself.

### E2b, E2c: chunked writes against a bounded queue
- **E2b:** the body is written in 256 KiB chunks with a flush after each.
- **E2c:** `NetworkListener.setMaxPendingBytes(512 KiB)` on every listener. A write to a client that does not read now blocks, holds its lease, and ends at the transport write timeout (10 s, fix 24 D2).

### E2d: terminate the connection on a failed write
- If the chunked write fails (IOException, including the write timeout), the Grizzly connection is terminated (`terminateWithReason`) before the exception is rethrown. The lease is returned as before.
- Why: Grizzly's `close()` is graceful and waits until the queued bytes are sent.
- Rack, dev-24b26 without E2d: after the 10 s timeout the sockets stayed ESTABLISHED with unsent data until the client closed, 85-120 s later.

### E2e: reset instead of FIN on the terminate (dev-24b26f)
- Rack B2R on dev-24b26e: with E2d, each timed-out socket goes ESTAB → FIN-WAIT-1 within 1 s and the response thread is freed (16/16 idle; pass on the thread side). But the socket keeps 195-201 KB of Send-Q until the client closes (~T+122 s). N240 would hold about 2,400 such sockets × ~200 KB of kernel memory.
- Fix: before the terminate, `SO_LINGER 0` on the SocketChannel (`ChunkedBody.abortiveClose`), so the close is a reset. The kernel drops the unread send buffer and the FIN-WAIT entry at once.
- Risk: this runs only after the write has failed or timed out, so nothing in that buffer would be read. If the option cannot be set, the terminate still closes gracefully, as in E2d.

### E1: skip the render for a client that is gone
- On the E0 list endpoints (view resources and storage pools, volumes, per-node storage pools), the render is skipped if the client's connection is already closed when the data arrives. The request is cancelled without an error report.

### E3, E3b: admission before the render, at the endpoint entry
- On the four list endpoints, the request is admitted against the budget at the endpoint entry (E3b), before the satellite fan-out (`fetchVlmAllocated`, `fetchThinFreeSpaceInfo`) and the object walk (`assembleList`). Both are lazy (`ScopeRunner` defers), and `RequestHelper.offWorker(flux, request)` admits inside `Flux.defer` before subscribing to them.
- A refusal is an immediate 503 + Retry-After 5: no fan-out, no walk under the read locks, no render.
- Why E3: under N240 with the budget only, 1,227 answers of 20.3 MB were rendered and then refused (about 25 GB in 20 min). That drove back-to-back Full GCs.
- Why E3b: the read locks (NODES_MAP + RSC_DFN_MAP, fair) cover the walk, not the render (ScopeRunner lock/unlock around the callable; the render runs later on API copies). A satellite's free-space update needs NODES_MAP write and waits behind every walk in progress. E3 admitted after the walk, so it capped renders but not walks.
- The ticket lives from the entry until the flux terminates, which covers fan-out, walk and render. A slow satellite fan-out (up to the 30 s fan-out timeout of fix 24 A) holds the ticket for that time.
- Refused when:
  - the client has its maximum of renders in flight (default 8 = 2 × the count, `..._PER_CLIENT_RENDERS`), or
  - the last answer for the same client + path?query was large and another of that size does not fit the client's count, its bytes or the global budget.
- A predicted-large admission **reserves** the predicted bytes and one count for the duration of the render, so parallel renders see each other (review); that is the expensive part E3 is about.
  - In the usual order, the ticket closes when the Flux terminates, right after the render and before doFlux's scheduler hop to the write. That drops the reservation, and the filter then takes the lease with a plain `tryAcquire`.
  - Only if the filter runs first does it convert the reservation into the lease of the real size in one step (`RenderTicket.toLease`), or drop it for a small answer.
  - The gap between render end and lease is the scheduler hop (milliseconds); the filter's lease check still applies there.
- Every 2xx answer's size is recorded (small ones too), so a target whose answer shrank stops being gated (review). Bounded LRU of 4096 client+target entries.
- Small or never-seen answers are limited only by the renders cap.

### E3c, E3d: ticket safety net and log lines (dev-24b26e)
- **E3c:** a render ticket older than 120 s is released on the next admission, with a WARN line describing the client, and counted (`getExpiredTicketCount`).
  - Why: a walk can wait on the fair read locks behind a long writer with its ticket held.
  - The before-render refusal warning describes the client: renders in flight, large answers and bytes held, and the global use.
- **E3d** (rack observability), all at INFO; the REST log id ties the lines of one request together:
  - `REST render admitted: <client> <path?query>, predicted <bytes|unknown>, renders in flight <n>` and `REST render released: … after <ms> ms, renders in flight <n>`, for targets predicted large or never seen. Predicted-small answers (polling) stay quiet.
  - `RequestVlmAllocated fan-out: <nodes>`, or `none: no reachable node with thin volumes matching the filters`, per fetch; the periodic UpdateSpaceInfoTask also logs one.
  - `RequestVlmAllocated answer from <node>: <signal> after <ms> ms` per node: at INFO only when slower than 1 s or not completed normally, otherwise DEBUG (review: 1 + N lines per list request would flood the log under a polling client).
  - Expired-ticket warnings are logged outside the budget monitor (review).

### E3e: refusals answer 503, not 500 (dev-24b26f)
- Rack B2R on dev-24b26e: round 2 (target known) gave 200 × 6 and **500** × 14 in 33-49 ms. The refusal before the fan-out was fast, but had the wrong status.
- Cause: `ApiCallRcRestUtils.toResponse(rc, status)` uses the given status only for a success. With an error entry (`FAIL_UNKNOWN_ERROR`) it answers 500.
  - This hit the E3/E3b refusal branch in `RequestHelper.resumeWithError`, and also fix 24's "response queue full" branch: that 503 had been a 500 since fix 24, and its test checked only `isRejected()`.
  - The filter's after-render refusal sets the status itself and was fine (503 × 9 in round 1).
- Fix: `RequestHelper.serviceUnavailable(rc)` builds the usual ApiCallRc body and sets 503 + `Retry-After: 5` explicitly; both branches use it. `Response.fromResponse(...)` keeps the entity, headers and media type; only the status line changes (review).
- Client-visible change: these refusals were 500 before (fix 24's queue-full since fix 24) and are 503 + Retry-After now. Clients that retry only on 503 now retry; the LINSTOR CLI and Incus treat both as errors.

## Known limits (documented, not changed)
- A never-seen large target still walks and renders once per client address + target before it can be predicted. Its first burst is capped only by the renders cap (8) per address; from the second round on, at most the count (4).
- The ticket max age (120 s) is a safety net, checked on the next admission, not on a timer. So is the lease max age (10 min), checked on acquire.
- The key is the client address. The two underlay addresses of one host count as two clients (2× per host), and clients behind one NAT or proxy share a bucket. The global budget caps both.
- The real size can exceed the reservation; the filter then checks the real size and may still refuse after the render.
- A stalled write blocks a RestResponse thread until the write timeout. That was already so with fix 24. The budget caps how many large answers can be stalled at once; the rest get 503.
- During the encode, the String and its bytes exist at the same time (transient, about 3 × the budgeted bytes per answer in flight).
- Only bodies over 1 MiB are budgeted.
- E1 only catches connections that are already closed. A client that stays connected and does not read is E2's job.
- E1 and E3/E3b cover the four E0 endpoints only. `GET /v1/resource-definitions/{rd}/resources` and other lists get only E2 (after the render).
- E2c applies to all traffic on the listener, including web UI files. Those writes block per 512 KiB instead of queueing.
